# ADR 0016: Leads and WhatsApp ad attribution

Status: Accepted for Phase 07. Checkpoint 07A (this document's database foundation, migration `0006`) is implemented, including the corrections of its independent database review (listed below); checkpoint 07B (lead services and API, see "Checkpoint 07B") is implemented; checkpoint 07C (the reconciler, see "Checkpoint 07C") is implemented and undergoing independent review (not approved, not production-ready); the UI (07D) is not implemented. Builds on [ADR 0011](0011-database-foundation-conventions.md), [ADR 0013](0013-whatsapp-webhook-ingestion.md) and [ADR 0015](0015-staff-inbox.md). **Partially supersedes ADR 0011** (see decision 2).

## Context

Phase 04 stores a Click-to-WhatsApp referral as an attribution touch (`lead_attributions`, contact-level, `lead_id` NULL) and deliberately creates no lead. Phase 07 adds leads: creation, one lifecycle, history, and the link between a lead and the ad evidence that produced it. This ADR fixes the rules and says, for each one, whether the **database** enforces it (migration `0006`, proven by tests on real PostgreSQL) or the **application** must (to be built and tested in 07B and 07C). A lead is not a registered student, a referral is not marketing consent, and Facebook is never told apart from Instagram unless the provider says so.

## Decisions

### 1. Lifecycle

All ten statuses of `LEAD_STATUSES` stay. They are partitioned (`src/db/schema/enums.ts`):

| Group                                        | Statuses                                                           |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Open                                         | NEW, QUALIFIED, INTERESTED, REGISTRATION_STARTED, REGISTERED, PAID |
| Closed                                       | CONVERTED, NOT_INTERESTED, NO_RESPONSE, INVALID                    |
| Staff-settable in Phase 07                   | NEW, QUALIFIED, INTERESTED, NOT_INTERESTED, NO_RESPONSE, INVALID   |
| Reserved (registration and payment workflow) | REGISTRATION_STARTED, REGISTERED, PAID, CONVERTED                  |

The reserved statuses belong to the later registration and payment workflow (roadmap item 8). No Phase 07 staff action and no reconciler sets them. Staff (ADMIN and STAFF, `lead.edit`) may move a lead between the six settable statuses in any direction; reopening a closed lead is allowed when the contact has no other open lead.

**Database:** lead history can only record the staff-settable statuses (CHECK), and a status change cannot commit without its history event (decision 6). Together these mean that no UPDATE can put a lead into a reserved status, or move a lead out of one, until a later migration says how. **Not database-enforced:** which status a lead is INSERTED with (the application creates leads as NEW; a row that already has a reserved status can only predate migration 0006 or be inserted by something other than the application).

### 2. One open lead per contact (partially supersedes ADR 0011)

ADR 0011 said: "a contact may have multiple leads; no uniqueness constraint until offerings exist". That stays true for history: a contact may have any number of **closed** leads. What changes is that a contact may have **at most one open lead at a time**, enforced by the partial unique index `leads_org_contact_open_uidx` on `(organization_id, contact_id) WHERE status IN (the six open statuses)`.

Why now, although offerings still do not exist: Phase 07 creates leads automatically from ad referrals and lets staff create them by hand. Without a rule, two ad clicks, or an ad click and a staff member, would produce two open leads for the same person, and nothing in the data could say which one a later message, touch or status change belongs to. The rule is deliberately contact-level and **provisional**: when offerings (courses, batches) exist, a contact may legitimately have one open lead per offering, and this index is to be replaced by one on `(organization_id, contact_id, offering)` in a new migration. The list of open statuses is part of the index definition; changing which statuses count as open needs a migration.

### 3. `revision`, `closed_at`, `cycle_started_at`

- **`revision`** (integer, default 0) is the optimistic-concurrency token. A lead is **inserted at revision 0** (a trigger refuses any other value; the default alone would not be a rule), and a trigger requires it to increase by exactly 1 with every status change and never to change otherwise. A revision can therefore only be reached by that many recorded status changes. Staff changes compare the revision the screen saw (07B), which detects every intervening change, including one that returns to the same status (the gap that blocker 56 records for conversations). Linking a touch does not change the revision.
- **`closed_at`** is the start of the **current** closed period: NULL while the lead is open, set when it enters a closed status, unchanged while it moves between closed statuses, cleared when it is reopened. A CHECK ties it to the status group; a trigger keeps it from moving while the lead stays closed.
- **`cycle_started_at`** is the start of the current inquiry cycle: the initiating referral's receipt time for an automatic lead; the creation time for a manual lead (the column default); the reopening time when a closed lead is reopened. For an automatic lead the **database** sets it, from the initiating touch's `received_at`, when the lead is inserted (a value supplied by the writer is ignored). A trigger allows it to change only on a reopen, and then only to a time not before the closure. A CHECK requires `closed_at >= cycle_started_at`.
- **`initiating_attribution_id`** is the confirmed Meta ad referral an automatic lead was created from; NULL for every other lead. It is written by the INSERT of the lead and can never be changed afterwards (trigger); a composite foreign key ties it to a touch of the same contact and organization. It is the database's proof of how a lead came to exist (decision 5).
- Earlier closures are recoverable from `lead_events`: every status change has a STATUS event with its from/to statuses, its revision and its time (decision 6).

### 4. Automatic lead policy

- Only a **confirmed Meta ad referral** creates a lead automatically: a touch with `source_type = 'META_AD'` (the provider's documented `ad` value) that arrived with a message. `REFERRAL`, organic and unknown-source evidence never does.
- The initiating touch is linked to the new lead in the same transaction.
- A later qualifying META_AD touch may be linked to the contact's open lead when it belongs to that lead's current cycle (decision 5).
- A **manually created lead does not claim historical touches**: creating one links nothing. Its cycle starts at its creation, so only touches that arrive afterwards can join it.
- No student, registration, payment or consent record is ever created, and no source is inferred.

**Database:** a lead can name an initiating touch only in its own INSERT, and only a touch that is META_AD, has a message and is not linked to anything; the lead's cycle then starts at that touch's receipt time. The transaction cannot commit unless the lead has its CREATED event naming that touch and the touch ends up linked to that lead. Any link is refused unless the touch is META_AD with a message. **Application (07C):** deciding _whether_ a touch should create a lead at all (freshness, the contact's other leads, archived contacts), see decision 7. The database does not judge that: a writer that inserts a lead naming an old unlinked ad touch gets a lead whose cycle starts at that touch's receipt time.

### 5. Attribution across lead cycles

Attribution has two levels. **Contact-level:** every touch for the contact, exactly as received. **Lead-level:** only the touches linked to that lead. A touch is linked at most once and the link never changes.

A touch must not be attached to a lead cycle it predates. Two timestamps describe a touch, with different meanings, and neither is the time the advertisement was clicked:

- `lead_attributions.received_at`: when this platform received the webhook delivery. Our clock, but Meta redelivers for days, so it can be much later than the message.
- `messages.occurred_at`: the provider's timestamp of the message. Not our clock; it can be skewed or wrong (Phase 04 stores it exactly and only flags anomalies).

**Conservative rule when they disagree: the earlier of the two is the evidence time.** A touch belongs to a cycle only if `LEAST(received_at, occurred_at) >= cycle_started_at`. So a message that was sent long ago but delivered late is old evidence, and a provider timestamp in the future cannot make evidence we received earlier look new. The cost is accepted: evidence that _might_ be old stays at contact level instead of being attached to a newer cycle, and nothing is ever attached wrongly on the strength of one optimistic timestamp.

The one exception is the touch that created the lead: its cycle starts at its own receipt time, its provider timestamp is normally a few seconds earlier, and it is linked as part of the creation. For that touch only the receipt time is compared with the cycle start; nothing else is waived.

**What proves that a touch is the initiating one.** Not a history event: an event can be inserted at any time, so an event written later for a lead that already exists must not grant the exception (review finding H1). The proof is `leads.initiating_attribution_id`: it can only be given by the INSERT that creates the lead, it is immutable, and the database itself derives the lead's cycle start from that touch. No timestamp supplied by a writer takes part. The INSERT cannot commit without the CREATED event naming the same touch and without the touch being linked to the lead, so a lead that exists can never acquire an origin, and the exception can only ever be used by the transaction that creates the lead. A CREATED event must name exactly the lead's recorded origin (and none for a lead without one), so history cannot contradict it.

**Database** (guard trigger on `lead_attributions`, at link time): the lead must be open; the touch must not predate the cycle by the rule above; for the lead's initiating touch the receipt time alone is compared. After a reopen the new `cycle_started_at` applies, so evidence from before the reopening is not attached (the initiating touch included, should it ever be unlinked at that point). **Application (07C):** a new lead after a closed one is created only by a touch whose evidence time is later than the contact's latest `closed_at`; older touches that arrive late are skipped (`SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE`).

### 6. Lead history (`lead_events`)

Append-only (a trigger refuses UPDATE and DELETE). Columns: organization, lead, contact, kind, actor (NULL = the system), origin, from/to status, attribution id, revision, time.

| Kind         | Shape (CHECK)                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CREATED      | revision 0, no statuses; origin `MANUAL` with the staff member and no touch, or origin `AD_REFERRAL` with the initiating touch and no staff member |
| STATUS       | the staff member, from and to (different, both staff-settable), revision >= 1                                                                      |
| TOUCH_LINKED | the touch; no origin, statuses or revision                                                                                                         |

Integrity, all in the database:

- Composite foreign keys: `(organization, lead, contact)` to `leads`, `(organization, actor)` to memberships, `(organization, attribution, contact)` to `lead_attributions`. The lead and the touch of one event therefore belong to the same contact of the same organization.
- Partial unique indexes: one CREATED per lead; a touch creates at most one lead; one TOUCH_LINKED per touch; one STATUS event per lead revision.
- **A link and its history commit together.** A deferred constraint trigger refuses a COMMIT in which a touch was linked without its TOUCH_LINKED event for that lead, and an immediate trigger refuses a TOUCH_LINKED event for a touch that is not linked to that lead (so: link first, then the event, in one transaction).
- **A status change and its history commit together**, the same way: a deferred trigger requires the STATUS event with the exact from/to statuses and the revision produced; an immediate trigger refuses a STATUS event unless the lead is in that status at that revision.
- The CHECK constraints are written so that they cannot evaluate to NULL (a CHECK accepts NULL).

- A CREATED event names exactly the touch recorded by the lead's INSERT (`initiating_attribution_id`), and none for a lead without one. A lead created from a touch cannot commit without that event.

**No history is invented.** Leads and links that existed before migration 0006 get no events and no origin. **Not database-enforced:** that a lead created _without_ a touch has a CREATED event (the application writes the `MANUAL` one; a lead without it is possible only outside the application, and such an event may also be added to it later, which grants nothing).

### 7. Reconcile decisions (`lead_reconcile_decisions`)

The reconciler (07C) considers each META_AD touch once and records a durable outcome beside the evidence, never in it. One row per touch (primary key), referencing a touch of the same organization and contact.

| Decision               | Meaning                                                     | Terminal |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| `SKIPPED_STALE`        | older than the freshness window when first considered       | yes      |
| `SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE` | not newer than the contact's latest lead-cycle boundary     | yes      |
| `SKIPPED_CONVERTED`    | the contact has a CONVERTED lead: no automatic lead         | yes      |
| `SKIPPED_ARCHIVED`     | the contact is archived                                     | yes      |
| `RETRY`                | a transient failure; attempts 1-4, due at `next_attempt_at` | no       |
| `PARKED`               | the fifth failure; needs an operator                        | yes      |

Retry policy: at most five failed attempts, with backoff 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours between them; the fifth failure is `PARKED`. A touch that gets linked has no row: a `RETRY` row that was waiting for it is deleted by the transaction that links it, **after** the link (the order is link, DELETE, history event).

**Database:**

- the shape of each decision (CHECK); `last_error_code` is a short code, never a message;
- **a linked touch has no decision row, ever.** Every INSERT, UPDATE and DELETE of a decision first locks the touch (`FOR SHARE`) and reads its link: a decision is recorded or changed only for an unlinked touch, and a link cannot commit while a decision row for its touch exists (deferred check). A link and a decision change for the same touch are therefore always decided one after the other, whichever starts first;
- terminal rows can never be updated or deleted, and **a touch with a terminal decision can never be linked**;
- a `RETRY` row is deleted only when its touch is linked, which before COMMIT only the linking transaction can see. Deleting it earlier is refused, so the attempt count cannot be reset by deleting the row and inserting a new one;
- `attempts` never decrease;
- a retry is scheduled in the future **by the database clock** (`clock_timestamp()`, not the transaction's start and not the writer's clock) whenever a schedule is assigned: on INSERT, and on an UPDATE that changes `attempts` or `next_attempt_at`. A row that is waiting or already due stays valid, and an UPDATE that touches neither column is not compared with the clock.

**Not database-enforced:** the backoff intervals, and that every new schedule consumes an attempt (the reconciler's job, 07C).

**How a later manual lead interacts with skipped evidence.** A terminal decision was made under the business state of its time and is not revisited: skipped evidence is never reactivated and never attached, not by the reconciler and not by a manual lead (which claims no historical touches anyway). It remains visible as contact-level evidence. `SKIPPED_CONVERTED` in particular means "no automatic lead for this touch"; it does not block the contact. Converted history suppresses the automatic **creation** of a new lead, not legitimate future attribution: when staff explicitly create a lead for a converted contact, a META_AD touch that arrives **afterwards** is undecided, belongs to the new lead's cycle, and may be linked to it.

There is no "unpark" operation: moving a `PARKED` touch back into play needs an explicit, reviewed change (blocker register).

### 8. Protection of attribution evidence

A guard trigger on `lead_attributions` enforces, for every writer:

- a touch is inserted **unlinked** (the Phase 04 handler already does exactly that, and is unchanged);
- a touch is never deleted;
- nothing in a touch ever changes except `lead_id`, **once**, from NULL to a lead (the comparison covers every other column, including ones added later);
- a link is accepted only if the touch is META_AD with a message, has no terminal decision, the lead is open and belongs to the same contact (the existing composite foreign key), and the touch does not predate the cycle (decision 5);
- a link commits only together with its TOUCH_LINKED event and with no decision row left for the touch (decision 7).

Existing rows are not touched by the migration. A touch that was already linked before migration 0006 stays linked exactly as it was, with no history event invented for it, and from then on is protected like any other.

### 9. Lock order (design contract)

Every transaction that creates a lead, changes a lead's status, links a touch or records a decision takes locks in this order:

1. the contact-scoped advisory lock (`pg_advisory_xact_lock` on a key derived from organization and contact);
2. the lead row (`FOR UPDATE`);
3. the attribution row;
4. the reconcile decision row;
5. history insertion.

Nothing may lock an attribution row before it holds the contact's advisory lock; in particular the reconciler's candidate enumeration must read candidates without locking them. The Phase 04 inbound handler takes none of these locks and is not changed.

**Decision rows.** A writer that inserts, updates or deletes a decision row holds the contact lock and locks the touch (step 3) before it touches the decision row (step 4); a linker deletes a waiting `RETRY` row only after it has linked the touch. Both therefore take the touch before the decision row.

**What the triggers lock**, so that the rules hold even for a writer that ignores the contract:

| Trigger                                                | Lock it takes                                       | Order                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| link guard (`lead_attributions`, BEFORE UPDATE)        | the lead, `FOR SHARE`                               | touch, then lead (2 after 3)                                |
| decision guard (BEFORE INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE)         | the touch, `FOR SHARE`                              | on UPDATE and DELETE: decision row, then touch (4 before 3) |
| lead INSERT guard, lead history guard, deferred checks | none (they read immutable or own-transaction facts) | -                                                           |

A writer that holds the contact lock is not affected by the two inversions: nobody else is working on that contact. A writer that does not hold it can deadlock against a concurrent link (for example: one transaction links the touch and then deletes the `RETRY` row while another updates that row directly). PostgreSQL detects the deadlock and aborts one of the two; whichever survives, the committed state is valid. This is tested in both forms: with the contact lock the two writers are simply serialized, in either order.

For the reconciler (07C): candidates are walked by a bounded keyset scan over `(received_at, id)` using the partial index `lead_attributions_unlinked_ad_idx`, plus due retries from `lead_reconcile_decisions_due_idx`. A contact whose lock is busy is skipped and the scan continues past it, so repeatedly busy contacts cannot occupy the same first batch forever. **No run guarantees that every touch is processed**: a run is bounded, and nothing is guaranteed while the reconciler is not running.

The triggers assume READ COMMITTED, the application's isolation level.

### 10. Upgrade

Migration 0006 is additive and applies after 0005. It starts with a preflight that aborts (SQLSTATE 23505, nothing changed, nothing recorded) when any contact has more than one open lead; it never closes, merges or deletes a lead. The read-only preflight query is in the migration file and in `RUNBOOK_WHATSAPP.md`. No application code creates leads before this phase, so no populated database is expected to have such a contact, but one written by hand or by a test fixture could.

Backfill of the new columns only: `revision` 0; `closed_at` = `status_changed_at` for a lead already in a closed status; `cycle_started_at` = the earlier of `created_at` and `status_changed_at` (nothing earlier than the row's own creation is known); `initiating_attribution_id` stays NULL for every existing lead, including one that already has linked touches (no origin is invented). No other value is changed.

## Consequences

- The schema carries the rules, so 07B-07D cannot quietly drift from them; changing a rule (which statuses are settable, the evidence-time rule, who may link) needs a reviewed migration.
- Leads that predate 0006 have no history and no recorded origin, and their pre-existing links have no TOUCH_LINKED event.
- A lead's origin is a column of the lead as well as a history event; the two cannot disagree (trigger), but both must be written by whoever creates an automatic lead.
- A manual lead's creation time is trustworthy only because the services take it from the database clock after the lock; a direct writer can still backdate it (blocker register). Nothing in 07B writes leads except those two services.
- A lead in a reserved status is frozen until the registration workflow defines its transitions.
- Erasing personal data from lead history or attribution evidence later needs a deliberate migration that disables the triggers (as for inbox history).
- The triggers do not cover `TRUNCATE` or a table owner who disables them, like every other trigger in this schema.

## Checkpoint 07B: lead services and API

`src/modules/leads` (public API `@/modules/leads`) and six thin routes under `src/app/api/leads/**`. The module takes only `@/modules/access` for authorization; it imports no WhatsApp, messaging or inbox code, makes no network request and writes only `leads` and `lead_events` (static tests). It creates leads manually and changes their status; it never links, unlinks or decides attribution (that is the reconciler's, 07C), and it never sets a reserved status.

| Route                             | Permission                                       | Service                                                     |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/leads`                  | `lead.read`                                      | `listLeads`: keyset pages, filters, bounded search          |
| `POST /api/leads`                 | `lead.edit`                                      | `createManualLead`: 201, or 200 with the existing open lead |
| `GET /api/leads/{id}`             | `lead.read`, `contact.read`, `conversation.read` | `getLeadDetail`                                             |
| `GET /api/leads/{id}/events`      | the same three                                   | `listLeadEvents`: keyset pages, newest first                |
| `GET /api/leads/for-contact/{id}` | `lead.read`, `contact.read`                      | `getContactLeads`: the open lead and recent leads           |
| `POST /api/leads/{id}/status`     | `lead.edit`                                      | `changeLeadStatus`: compare-and-set on the revision         |

**Trustworthy lifecycle timestamps (the 07A reviewer's condition).** The database accepts any `cycle_started_at` for a manual lead (decision 3 enforces the transitions, not the first value), so the services are the guarantee. The client sends a contact id (creation) or a target status and the revision it saw (status change); every other key, including `cycleStartedAt`, `closedAt`, `statusChangedAt`, `createdAt`, `revision`, `origin` and any attribution, is refused by the strict schemas (400, the key name only). The services read ONE value of `clock_timestamp()` **after** the contact's advisory lock (and, for a status change, the lead row lock): the **action time**. `now()` (the transaction start) and JavaScript time are never used, so a request that waited for a lock is not stamped with the time it started waiting. Tested with a real held lock and a delay measured on the database clock.

Three different things must not be confused:

| What                                | Which columns                                                                          | Value                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Actual database action time**     | `status_changed_at`, `updated_at`, and on creation `created_at` and `cycle_started_at` | Exactly the action time above, always.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Status history timestamp**        | `lead_events.created_at` (CREATED and STATUS rows)                                     | Exactly the action time above, always: the history says when the action really happened, even when a lifecycle boundary below was held back.                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Conservative lifecycle boundary** | `closed_at`; `cycle_started_at` after a **reopening**                                  | The action time, unless a stored time is ahead of the database clock (an automatic lead's cycle starts at the touch's receipt time, which a skewed provider or platform clock can place in the future). Then `closed_at = GREATEST(action time, cycle_started_at)` and a reopened `cycle_started_at = GREATEST(action time, closed_at)`. |

So `closed_at` (and a reopened cycle start) may be later than `status_changed_at` and the history time of the same change; they are never earlier. In the normal case the action time, the status time and the history time coincide; the boundaries can differ only through the accepted `GREATEST` behaviour, when a stored time is in the future of the database clock (every manual lead has times in the past, so for it all of the values coincide). The effect of a **raised** boundary is the opposite of "making a touch look newer": a touch is compared with the boundary, so a raised boundary makes a touch look **older relative to it**, and a touch that genuinely arrived inside the real cycle can be judged to predate the boundary and be **excluded** (it stays contact-level evidence; automatic handling records it as `SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE`). That is the conservative direction: it can lose legitimate attribution, it can never attach evidence to a cycle it predates, and it keeps `closed_at >= cycle_started_at` and a reopened cycle from starting before the closure. A move between **closed** statuses (for example `NO_RESPONSE` to `NOT_INTERESTED`) is not a new closure: it keeps the earlier `closed_at` and the cycle start, so the closure boundary stays the time the lead first closed. Tests: "status changes" in `workflow.db.test.ts` (equal in the normal case; `closed_at >= cycle_started_at` and a never-earlier reopening when a stored time is ahead; closed-to-closed keeps the boundary).

**Manual creation.** Advisory lock, contact `FOR SHARE` (it cannot be archived in between), existing open lead returned unchanged (`created: false`, no second lead, no duplicate history), archived contact refused (`CONTACT_ARCHIVED`), then the lead (NEW, revision 0, no touch, `initiating_attribution_id` NULL) and its `CREATED`/`MANUAL` event with the actor. It links no touch, records no consent and sends nothing. If the one-open-lead index fires (a writer that ignores the lock order), the creation is retried once and returns the lead that won.

**Status change.** Locks in the contract order: the actor's organization and membership (below), the contact's advisory lock, the lead row `FOR UPDATE`; then: stale revision refused (`STALE_STATE`, also when the status has come back to what the screen saw), a lead in a reserved status refused (`RESERVED_STATUS`), a change to the status it already has writes nothing and does not move the revision, a reopening refused while the contact has another open lead (`ALREADY_OPEN`, also mapped from the database's own unique-index error), then the update and the `STATUS` event. Closing sets `closed_at`; moving between closed statuses keeps it; reopening clears it and starts a new cycle at the reopening time. Both use `GREATEST` against the stored times so a cycle start slightly ahead of the database clock (a skewed receipt time) can never violate `closed_at >= cycle_started_at` or the reopening trigger.

**The actor is held, not only checked, in the staff-management order.** Inside the transaction a lead write first locks the actor's **organization** row `FOR KEY SHARE` (the identity comes from the authenticated context, never the client), then the actor's **membership** row `FOR SHARE`, then revalidates the role. Staff management (`suspendMember`, `changeMemberRole`, `reactivateMember`) locks the organization row `FOR UPDATE` and then the target membership `FOR UPDATE`: the same order, organization then membership, so they wait for each other and cannot deadlock. A suspension or demotion therefore waits until an in-flight lead write finishes: it either commits before the write looks (the write is refused) or after it commits (the write was made while the actor was allowed), and a completed suspension prevents every later write. `FOR KEY SHARE` is the weakest row lock and is exactly what the later INSERTs take on the organization through their foreign keys, so nothing new is held; lead writes do not block each other on it. The 07A triggers add no organization or membership locks (they lock only lead, attribution and contact rows; the actor foreign key of a history row takes `KEY SHARE` on a membership the writer already holds `FOR SHARE`).

**Reads.** Everything is scoped by `ctx.organizationId`; the contact's leads return the newest ten as history and look the **open** lead up on its own (it may be older than the ten), so `openLead` and `can.create` always follow the database; another organization's id is the same 404 as a missing one (same body, byte for byte). The list is one statement (contact and source summary joined, no N+1); keyset pages use microsecond text like the inbox. The detail keeps **evidence linked to this lead** apart from **other evidence recorded for the contact** (unlinked, or linked to another lead); an unlinked touch is never evidence for a lead. A lead without linked evidence reads "No advertisement referral recorded", never "organic"; `META_AD` reads "Meta ad", and Facebook is never told apart from Instagram. Provider text (headlines, ad text, ids, links, names) is cleaned, bounded plain text; links are reduced to origin and path (no query or fragment); the click id is reduced to its last four characters; `provider_data` and any storage key are never returned; nothing is fetched. Small display helpers are repeated in the module on purpose (modules stay independent; Phase 06 is not refactored).

**Not in 07B.** Automatic creation and every link or decision (07C), the `/leads` page and the inbox lead card (07D), an operator procedure for skipped evidence, rate limiting of the lead endpoints (blocker register).

## Checkpoint 07C: the automatic lead reconciler

`src/modules/leads/reconcile` and the command `npm run leads:reconcile`. It runs AFTER Phase 04 has committed a message and its attribution touch (the inbound handler is unchanged: it still inserts the touch with `lead_id` NULL and creates no lead). It considers **confirmed Meta ad touches whose message is INBOUND and belongs to the touch's own contact** only: the message that carried the touch decides, and an attribution row tied to an outbound message (which Phase 04 never writes) is never read, linked, decided, counted as work, or allowed to hold anything back. **Message ownership (evidence-integrity review).** The schema ties a touch to a message of the same ORGANIZATION only (`(organization_id, message_id)` → `messages`), so a touch of contact B can name an inbound message of contact A; the database accepts such a row. Ownership is proved through the message's conversation (`messages.conversation_id` → `conversations.contact_id` equal to the touch's `contact_id`, within the touch's organization): a matching message id proves nothing about the contact. The check is applied in every place a touch's message is judged: the candidate scan (inside the existing `LATERAL ... LIMIT 1` probe, one row at most, order and limit unchanged), the revalidation under the locks while handling (a touch that fails it is `gone`, so it is never linked to an open lead and never initiates a lead), failure recording (no `RETRY` or `PARKED` is created or advanced for it), the first-touch hold (an earlier wrong-contact `RETRY` holds nothing back) and the remaining-work counts. Nothing is repaired: the evidence's `contact_id` and `message_id` are never changed, no terminal decision is invented for it, and a `RETRY` or `PARKED` row already stored for such a touch (none can be written by this code) is left as it is and excluded from the counts. Different WhatsApp accounts are not a restriction: a contact's evidence on any account of the organization is eligible. Every other source (REFERRAL, organic, website, QR, manual, anything unknown) is likewise never considered, and Facebook is never told apart from Instagram. It is opt-in in every way: `LEADS_RECONCILER_ENABLED=true`, started by nothing else (it is not exported from `@/modules/leads`), no scheduler. It calls no Meta API, sends nothing, writes no consent, student or payment record, and never changes provider evidence (the only change to a touch is its link). **It is not production-ready reconciliation**: there is no scheduler, supervision or alerting (blocker register), and a run is bounded and never promises to finish everything.

**Policy, in order** (pure function `decide`, `reconcile/policy.ts`; every comparison of times is made by PostgreSQL on exact microsecond values, under the locks):

| Step | Condition                                                                                      | Result                                                                     |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1    | the contact is archived                                                                        | `SKIPPED_ARCHIVED`                                                         |
| 2    | the touch's evidence time is older than the freshness window at its first consideration        | `SKIPPED_STALE`                                                            |
| 3    | the contact HAS an open lead and the touch predates the lead's current cycle                   | `SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE`                                                     |
| 4    | the contact has an open lead otherwise                                                         | link the touch to it **at once** (nothing waits for an earlier touch)      |
| 5    | no open lead, and the touch is not later than the latest closure of any of the contact's leads | `SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE`                                                     |
| 6    | no open lead and the contact has a CONVERTED lead                                              | `SKIPPED_CONVERTED` (converted history suppresses AUTOMATIC creation only) |
| 7    | no open lead and an earlier touch of the contact is in its retry back-off, within the hold     | wait (nothing written); see the first-touch policy                         |
| 8    | otherwise                                                                                      | create exactly one NEW lead that names the touch                           |

Skips are terminal (a trigger refuses to change them) and are never reactivated: not by a new lead, by un-archiving, or by a later run. **Evidence time** is the earlier of `lead_attributions.received_at` and the message's `occurred_at`, the conservative rule of decision 5: a late delivery of an old message is old evidence, and a provider time in the future cannot make evidence fresh or newer. The creation boundary is strict: a touch must be LATER than the latest closure, equal is a skip.

**Freshness (default 14 days, configurable 1-90; applies to linking to an open lead as well as to creation).** A touch is judged against the instant at which it was **first considered**, read from the database clock inside the attempt's transaction **after** the organization lock and the contact's advisory lock were obtained (never from JavaScript, the command line or the environment). If that attempt fails recoverably, the instant is stored with the failure, **in the same transaction and while the contact is still held**, as the `created_at` of the touch's RETRY row, and **every later attempt judges freshness against that stored instant**: a touch that was fresh when first considered stays eligible through valid retries even if the retry happens after the window has passed; a failure recorded later cannot move the anchor forward, and an existing RETRY row keeps its original anchor. A second reconciler cannot judge the touch in between (see "One attempt, one transaction, one savepoint" below). What is NOT protected: a touch that is only passed over (busy, held back by the policy) has no stored anchor and is judged afresh at its next attempt; a failure that cannot be recorded (a lost connection or server crash; or a lock timeout while recording) leaves no anchor either; and a service outage longer than the window turns old evidence into `SKIPPED_STALE`, which is terminal (a skip is never silently resurrected). Choose the window deliberately before the first run on a backlog.

**Archived contacts.** `SKIPPED_ARCHIVED` is terminal. Un-archiving a contact later does not reactivate its skipped touches (they stay contact-level evidence), and nothing here implements an un-archive workflow or rewrites evidence. A touch RECEIVED after the contact is un-archived is an ordinary new touch and is considered normally.

**The historical cycle boundary, and why the approved schema is enough.** The question is what "the contact's latest closure" is when a lead has reopened and its `closed_at` is NULL again. (a) When the contact has an OPEN lead nothing is created, and the boundary for linking is that lead's `cycle_started_at`: a trigger guarantees a reopened cycle never starts before the closure it follows, so it already carries every earlier closure, and the database enforces the same comparison at link time. (b) When the contact has NO open lead every lead is closed and each has its current `closed_at`; a lead's `closed_at` is the start of its current closed period and only moves forward (it is kept across closed-to-closed moves and replaced only by a later closure), so the maximum over the contact's leads is the latest closure of all of them, reopened leads included. `lead_events` is not needed for this decision, and it is not used (its STATUS rows record the action time, which can differ from the raised lifecycle boundary). The one gap is **leads that predate migration 0006**: a lead that was closed and reopened before the migration has no history and a `cycle_started_at` backfilled from its creation, so an older closure of it is unknowable; nothing is invented (blocker register).

**First-touch policy (APPROVED by the product owner after the first 07C review; the earlier "wait for any earlier undecided touch" behaviour was never approved and is gone).** Receipt order is not proof of the order of the ad clicks, so nothing here claims exact first-click attribution.

- **Existing open lead:** nothing waits. An eligible touch is linked immediately, subject to freshness, the cycle rule and the database's rules.
- **New lead:** an earlier touch of the contact that has an actual RETRY decision may hold a later touch back from INITIATING the lead, for at most **15 minutes measured from the earlier touch's trusted first-consideration time** (the anchor above), so that a transient fault on it does not at once hand the lead to a later touch. After the hold a later touch may initiate the lead on a subsequent run. The hold is a policy, **not a guaranteed latency**: nothing schedules the reconciler, so a held touch is processed when the next run happens.
- An earlier touch that was **never considered** imposes no hold (it has no decision; in a run it is simply reached first because candidates are in receipt order), and an earlier touch of an **outbound** message never does.
- **Historical evidence is not forced:** when a later touch initiates the lead, the earlier touch is evaluated under the normal cycle rule on its retry and, if it predates the new cycle, gets the correct terminal decision (`SKIPPED_BEFORE_CYCLE`); it is never force-linked.

**Per touch, one transaction, one lock order** (the contract of decision 9; the reconciler has no staff actor, so no membership lock): the organization row `FOR KEY SHARE` (the same side as a lead INSERT's foreign key and as staff management, which takes the organization `FOR UPDATE` first, so suspending or demoting staff cannot deadlock with it); the contact's advisory lock, **tried**; the contact row `FOR SHARE` and the open lead `FOR UPDATE`; only then the touch row `FOR UPDATE` and its decision row `FOR UPDATE`; then the writes. **No lock is waited for:** every row lock is `NOWAIT` and the advisory lock is only tried, so a contact or row in use is passed over at once as "busy" (SQLSTATE 55P03, never a failure and never an attempt). The scan locks no attribution, lead, decision, contact or message row and takes no advisory lock; the one lock it takes is the organization row `FOR KEY SHARE ... SKIP LOCKED`, inside its own short transaction (below). A candidate is a pointer only: its eligibility (still unlinked, still a Meta ad on an inbound message, still no terminal decision, a RETRY still due, the facts above) is **re-read under the locks**.

**One attempt, one transaction, one savepoint (the freshness race, second review C-M2).** A recoverable failure must not give another reconciler a window in which it can judge the same touch stale before the first one has recorded its original eligibility. So the attempt and the recording of its failure are **one** PostgreSQL transaction:

1. `BEGIN`, with `lock_timeout` and `statement_timeout` set transaction-locally;
2. the organization row `FOR KEY SHARE NOWAIT` and the contact's advisory lock (tried), **both before the savepoint**;
3. the first-consideration instant, read from the database clock while the contact is held;
4. `SAVEPOINT`, then the fallible work: the contact, open lead, touch and decision rows revalidated and locked `NOWAIT`, the facts, the decision or the lead with its history and link, then `SET CONSTRAINTS ALL IMMEDIATE` so that the **deferred** history triggers of migration 0006 (which would otherwise fire only at `COMMIT`, outside any savepoint) run inside the savepoint and their failures are recoverable too;
5. on a recoverable failure: `ROLLBACK TO SAVEPOINT`, then, **still holding the contact**, the touch row and the decision row are locked again in the approved order and the `RETRY` (attempt 1-4) or `PARKED` (attempt 5) row is written with the original anchor; then `COMMIT`.

Which locks survive the rollback to the savepoint: the transaction-level advisory lock and the organization row lock were acquired **before** it, so they survive; every row lock taken after it (contact, lead, touch, decision) and every write after it are released or undone and are taken again by the recording. A test holds a third session queued for the contact's lock across the whole failure and shows it is not granted until the attempt's transaction commits.

Failure classes: (A) a **recoverable statement failure** (a constraint, a trigger, a statement timeout inside the work, a deferred constraint) is recorded as above; (B) **lock contention** (SQLSTATE 55P03: `NOWAIT`, the lock timeout) is "busy", never a failure and never an attempt, and a failed `NOWAIT` on the organization is "organization busy"; (C) **loss of the transaction or the connection** (a killed backend, a crash, a network failure) loses the transaction and the lock with it: the command then records the failure in a transaction of its own with the anchor it had read, so the anchor survives **when the process survives**, and is lost with the process otherwise; in the gap between the loss and that recording another reconciler could judge the touch, and a lost process leaves no anchor at all, which cannot be closed without independently durable state. A recoverable failure (class A) keeps its anchor and its contact serialization; the **loss of an uncommitted anchor** (class C) is a different thing and is not recoverable; (D) a **commit-time failure** that is not a deferred constraint (a lost connection during `COMMIT`) is class C **and its outcome is UNKNOWN** (next paragraph); deferred constraint failures are not commit-time failures any more, because `SET CONSTRAINTS ALL IMMEDIATE` raises them inside the savepoint. No unconditional guarantee is made for class C.

**An ambiguous COMMIT is unknown, never assumed rolled back (third review, M1).** The acknowledgement of a `COMMIT` can be lost after the server committed: a `RETRY` row, a decision, or a whole lead with its link may exist although the caller saw an error (reproduced with SQLSTATE 08006: three actual failures recorded as attempts 2, 4 and `PARKED`). So the fallback never records "one more failure" on faith. Under the contact lock, before any fallible work, the attempt **observes** the touch's state: no decision, a `RETRY` with N attempts, a terminal decision, or already linked (a terminal or linked touch ends the attempt there). The fallback takes the locks again in the approved order, reads the committed state, and: a linked touch or a terminal decision is **never** touched and no `RETRY` is created for a link (the attempt's own commit may have landed, or another worker finished the touch); a state **equal** to the observed one proves that no transition committed, so exactly one attempt is recorded, with the original anchor; a state that **differs** (the attempt's own `RETRY` landed, or another worker advanced the touch) writes nothing (`changed`); a failure before any state was observed writes nothing (`unknown`). Both are reported as `failuresUncertain`. Because every state change moves a touch forward only (absent, then `RETRY` with growing attempts, then terminal or linked) and every writer holds the contact lock, equality of the observed and the committed state is conclusive. **One actual processing failure therefore consumes at most one attempt; the cost of the uncertainty is that a failure whose recording is ambiguous may be counted once or not at all, never twice.** A fallback whose own `COMMIT` is lost is not repeated (`failuresUnrecorded`). An attempt that does nothing (busy, waiting) and then loses its connection is treated like any other failure of an observed, unchanged touch.

**Query bounds.** Every statement the reconciler issues (candidate scan, remaining-work count, the readiness check of the command, handling a touch, recording a failure) runs in a transaction that first sets PostgreSQL's own `lock_timeout` (default 3 s) and `statement_timeout` (default 20 s, lock waits included) with `set_config(..., is_local => true)`: the database cancels the statement, not a JavaScript timer, and nothing is left on a pooled connection afterwards. Defined limits: the **run budget** (default 30 s: ADMISSION control, see below), the **per-statement timeouts** above, the **scan limit** (default 5,000 candidates EXAMINED per run: candidates the scan RETURNED and the run attempted or passed over; the rows PostgreSQL itself looks at to find them are not counted, see below) and the **batch** (default 100 touches processed per run). **The run budget and the batch limit admit work; they are not a deadline** (third review, M2). Both are checked **before every candidate scan, every new page and every candidate**; a run that has used one up starts none of these and reports `time_limit` or `batch_limit`. A statement already admitted is not interrupted by the budget: it is bounded by the two database limits and finishes or fails, then the run stops; one run can therefore last **longer than its budget**: by the bounded statements already in flight (a scan or a count waiting on a table lock lasts up to `lock_timeout`, a statement up to `statement_timeout`) and by the final remaining-work count, which is always attempted afterwards under the same timeouts. No maximum elapsed time, no maximum processing latency and no maximum retry delay is promised. A timed-out scan ends the run as `query_failed` (exit 1), the count then reads as unknown, and only fixed codes (`pg_55p03`, `pg_57014`) appear in the report. A blocked readiness check of the command ends as `database_timeout`. **What waits and what does not.** (A) Processing a candidate waits for nothing: the contact lock is tried and every row lock is `NOWAIT`. (B) Recording a failure is the one place that may WAIT for a lock: in the attempt's own transaction only for the touch and decision rows, which the contract says nobody else holds, and in the fallback (class C) for the organization, the contact and those rows; every wait is bounded by `lock_timeout`, because an unrecorded failure loses the freshness anchor. A recording that times out is reported as `failuresUnrecorded`. (C) The final remaining-work count also runs in a transaction with both timeouts and, if it fails, is reported as unknown. A **table-level** lock (a migration) can still make a statement wait, and `lock_timeout` ends that wait.

**What a touch causes**, all in that one transaction and committed together or not at all: a link: the touch's `lead_id`, the removal of its RETRY row (after the link, the order the triggers require), a `TOUCH_LINKED` event; a creation: the lead (NEW, revision 0, `initiating_attribution_id` = the touch, `cycle_started_at` derived by the database from the touch's receipt time, never from the reconciler), its `CREATED`/`AD_REFERRAL` event with no actor, then the link as above. The 07A triggers are the final check on all of it, and nothing here manufactures the initiating-touch exception for an existing lead: only a lead's own INSERT can name its origin. `CREATED` carries the action time and `TOUCH_LINKED` a later reading of the database clock, so history reads in the order things happened.

**Retry.** Any recoverable failure of the attempt (a constraint, a trigger, a statement timeout) rolls its work back entirely and is recorded as the next step, **in the attempt's own transaction** (a transaction of its own only in the fallback of failure class C): `RETRY` with attempts 1-4 and a due time 1 minute, 5 minutes, 30 minutes, 2 hours ahead (the database clock; these are the nominal back-off intervals, not a guaranteed elapsed time: a run happens when someone starts one), then `PARKED` at the fifth failed attempt (final). The recorded code is a SQLSTATE or `internal_error`, never a message. A busy contact is not a failure and costs no attempt. A RETRY that is linked loses its row in the linking transaction; one that ends in a skip keeps its attempt count.

**Progress across runs (fairness).** The accepted pre-production contract: **compliant application writers must not repeatedly starve unrelated eligible contacts or organizations merely because older contacts or organizations are temporarily busy.** Universal fairness is NOT claimed, and a larger scan limit does not guarantee eventual progress. Candidates are scanned oldest first by `(received_at, id)` on `lead_attributions_unlinked_ad_idx`, in pages, and the filters that matter for starvation are in the SQL itself, so none of these occupies the scan budget: a touch with a terminal decision (skipped, parked); a RETRY that is not due; a touch whose contact's **advisory lock is held by another session of this database right now** (read from `pg_locks`, restricted to the current database's OID, using the same key the lead writers take, so an identical key held in another database of the cluster hides nothing); a touch that the first-touch hold keeps back; any touch of an outbound message; and a touch of a **busy organization** (next paragraph).

_Organizations (third review, M3: the scan-level lock)._ Staff management (suspension, demotion, reactivation, creation) holds an organization row `FOR UPDATE` for the length of its transaction, which makes every attempt on that organization busy. The candidate scan therefore asks PostgreSQL for each candidate's organization row `FOR KEY SHARE ... SKIP LOCKED` **inside the scan statement**: a row that a staff transaction holds conflicts and is skipped, the candidate is **not returned and does not use up the `LIMIT`**, however many organizations are busy; a free organization's row is locked `KEY SHARE`, which is the same lock the attempt takes anyway and conflicts only with an exclusive writer. This replaces the earlier probes (a per-organization `NOWAIT` transaction, a per-run exclusion list and a probe limit of 100, which is what let more than 100 busy organizations starve the work behind them: reproduced with 101 busy organizations and scan limit 1, `fetched` 101, `scanned` 1, `processed` 0 on every separate run). No state is kept between runs, no file, no cursor, no table, no migration; the retired report fields `organizationsUnavailable` and `deferredByOrganization` (and the internal probe limit) are gone because SKIP LOCKED does not say how many rows it skipped, and **a number that cannot be measured is not reported**.

_What the scan locks, and for how long._ The organization rows of the candidates it examines, `KEY SHARE`, and nothing else: no attribution, lead, decision, contact or message row and no advisory lock (tested from a second session while a scan transaction is open). The locks end with the scan's own transaction, **before any candidate is handled**; nothing is held across the per-candidate transactions, each of which takes the organization lock, the contact's advisory lock and the row locks again in the contract order. The scan never waits for a row lock (SKIP LOCKED); like every statement of the module it can wait only for a table-level lock (a migration), which `lock_timeout` ends. It cannot invert the lock order because it holds nothing else, and a staff transaction that starts while a scan is running waits for the scan's few milliseconds, never the other way round. Many concurrent scans take compatible `KEY SHARE` locks (PostgreSQL may record them as a multixact: a small cost, not a conflict).

_How it is written, and why._ The lock is a correlated `EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM organizations o WHERE o.id = a.organization_id FOR KEY SHARE SKIP LOCKED)` filter on the attribution row, and the inbound-message check is a `LATERAL ... LIMIT 1` probe, both inside the candidate statement (`candidateQuery`, exported so a test can EXPLAIN exactly what runs). Two findings from running the ACTUAL statement on disposable data (60,000 touches, 40 organizations, 30 of them held `FOR UPDATE`, local PostgreSQL 17, not a production-scale certification): (1) the literal form `JOIN organizations ... FOR KEY SHARE OF o SKIP LOCKED` is correct (busy organizations are skipped before the limit) but PostgreSQL then puts the locking step above the whole join tree, so every candidate of a busy organization goes through all the joins first (about 1.1 million buffers and 2.9 s against about 11,000 buffers and 60 ms without the lock at 15,000 touches); as a filter on the attribution row the lock is asked for before the joins and the `LIMIT` still stops the ordered index scan early; (2) with the lock in place the planner's estimate shifted and, for the second page, it nested-looped over a full scan of `messages` for each candidate (405 million rows removed by a join filter, 30 s, above the 20 s statement timeout). That weakness was already latent in the earlier statement (a full scan of `messages` per candidate at 200 candidates), and the lateral probe, which PostgreSQL cannot turn into a scan, removes it: page 1 about 0.2 s and page 2 about 0.08 s with 30 of 40 organizations busy, and 0.19 s per page with none busy. Eligibility is unchanged (still an INBOUND message that exists for the touch).

_Rows examined, candidates returned, touches processed._ Three different numbers. PostgreSQL examines index entries in `(received_at, id)` order and drops those that are not candidates (a busy organization or contact, a decided or held-back touch); that number is **not bounded by the scan limit** (a run's scan statement is bounded by `statement_timeout`, and costs more the more such rows lie ahead of the first eligible one: 44,693 rows were dropped for the first page in the 30-of-40 measurement) and it is not reported. The scan RETURNS at most the page size (`fetched`). The run examines those candidates (`scanned`, bounded by the scan limit) and PROCESSES (decides, links, creates, records a failure) at most the batch size (`processed`).

Because busy organizations, busy contacts and held-back touches are dropped inside the query, repeated runs, including separate processes of the command, reach the eligible work behind them. The `pg_locks` check and the organization lock are **point-in-time observations** (a lock can be taken a moment after the scan): the attempt still takes the organization lock `NOWAIT`, TRIES the contact lock and takes every row lock `NOWAIT`, which is the authority. An organization that turns busy AFTER the scan makes the attempts on it `busy` (one cheap attempt per candidate, nothing waited for; the contact's later touches are passed over for the rest of that run so an inquiry never overtakes an earlier one) and the next scan skips the organization.

**The conditional fairness guarantee (verified locally on disposable databases, not a general proof).** For a stable candidate set, when older organizations are locked by compliant application services (staff management) at scan time, those locks do not prevent available later organizations from being considered in repeated bounded runs, however many organizations are busy and without any state between runs; and once the locks are released the previously skipped touches are processed. Verified with: 101 busy organizations, scan limit 1, separate processes of the command; the real suspension, demotion and reactivation services; concurrent reconcilers; busy contacts; manual creation, closure and reopening. **Not claimed:** universal starvation freedom, a processing latency, exact first-ad-click attribution, fairness against an arbitrary external writer, or progress while the database is unavailable. If repeated normal application operations still showed starvation, that would be a defect to report, not a limitation to accept.

**Accepted residual limitations** (documented, not solved): (1) a prefix of at least `scanLimit` candidates whose touch, contact, lead or decision rows are locked by a **noncompliant external writer** (one that does not follow the lock contract: no writer in this code base is one) is examined and passed over at a cost of milliseconds each, and would use the run's scan budget; raising `LEADS_RECONCILE_SCAN_LIMIT` makes that less likely but does not guarantee progress; (2) staff-management transactions that stay open for a long time keep their organization unavailable for as long as they last (the reconciler never waits for them and never forces them); (3) a large number of dropped rows ahead of the first eligible one makes each scan statement slower (bounded by `statement_timeout`; measured locally only); (4) nothing is persisted between runs, so a candidate that is busy at every scan is simply tried again next time. A **durable** scan position would remove (1) but needs a persistent cursor, a schema addition and a new migration, which is not made without approval. Counters keep the reason a candidate was passed over: `busy` (lock contention) and `waiting` (the policy hold) are never mixed, no candidate is counted twice (`scanned = processed + busy + waiting + conflicts + gone`), and `fetched` (candidates returned by the scan), `scanned` (candidates examined) and `processed` (touches that received a decision, a link, a lead, or a recorded failure) are three different numbers. `remaining` reports `due`, `retryWaiting` and `parked` as counts of the **eligible population only**: confirmed Meta ad touches of inbound messages that are unlinked; a decision row tied to an outbound message, to another source type or to a touch without a message is never counted, and those rows are not processed.

**Not in 07C:** the leads page and the inbox card (07D), any scheduling or supervision, an operator procedure to review or release skipped or parked evidence (and for any invalid legacy attribution row tied to an outbound message: such a row stays untouched contact-level evidence and can be listed with a read-only query, see the runbook), and any use of provider text beyond the evidence already stored.

## Rejected alternatives

- Creating leads inside the Phase 04 inbound handler (changes a verified handler and puts lead failures next to message storage).
- A lead for every new contact (most contacts are existing students and parents, not leads).
- Value-based status checks without a revision (cannot detect a change that returns to the same status).
- A "last touch wins" or timestamp-only attribution without per-touch links (cannot say which evidence belongs to which lead cycle).
- Trusting one timestamp (`received_at` alone would attach late-delivered old messages; `occurred_at` alone would trust a clock that is not ours).
- Letting a manual lead adopt earlier unlinked touches (attaches evidence to a cycle it may not belong to, and the link could never be undone).
- Editing or deleting attribution evidence, or a mutable link.
- Application-only enforcement of link and history atomicity (a single forgetful writer would make history untrustworthy).
